# Customer price demand

`src/demand-config.js` is the editable configuration for the three supplied curves, temperature boundaries, location/quality sensitivity, branding reductions/cap and baseline conversion. `src/demand.js` is the single implementation used by live sales and projections.

Each real visitor is routed through the existing competition probability, then makes one purchase-interest decision. Interested visitors still face the original capacity, queue length, patience and service-time constraints. Counts are never multiplied by a price factor and then checked against price again. Competitor diversion uses the existing strength and defenses; the permitted locations, weather/recipe traffic volumes, recipes, inventory, service, finances and bank are unchanged.

The baseline conversion is 0.60 before the existing taste modifier (`0.55 + 0.52 × quality`). For a perfect recipe that is 0.642 at $2. The average-weather $1 factor of 1.50 yields 0.963, preserving the meaningful difference between discounts while remaining below 100% conversion. There is no minimum purchase probability. Existing $0.75 pricing remains available, using the $1 curve value; negative prices are rejected. Interpolation is linear between adjacent points.

Snow or temperatures at/below 40°F select the cold curve, 85°F and above select hot, and intermediate temperatures select average. The existing weather foot-traffic multiplier remains separate. The previous extra rain conversion multiplier and price-specific temperature offset are removed; no extra temperature multiplier is applied to purchase probability. The existing temperature-sensitive recipe taste calculation is retained.

Sensitivity scales a factor's deviation from 1, in both directions. Downtown uses the supplied good/better/best thresholds (0.80/0.95). All weather-specific hard cutoffs take precedence over location and branding. Purchased branding upgrades stack, including previously purchased stand styles, up to 30%; operational equipment and house ownership add no brand recognition. This accumulated recognition persists when a different stand is equipped. Physical equipment benefits still depend on equipped items. Old flat branding additions to conversion are removed, so recognition is counted only through sensitivity; the existing competition-defense effects remain separate.

At opening, the game records the demand inputs/result in `dayRun.priceDemand` and freezes its probability and competitor probability in the saved session. Receipts use that day's recorded price. Saving/resuming does not recalculate demand, consume ingredients again or add a second permit. A legacy in-progress save without the new demand version retains its old frozen probability and original decision order until closing. Historical records without demand metadata receive no invented explanation.

End-of-day and historical-day feedback uses the recorded calculation, with concise explanations for discounts, higher prices, hot/cold weather and helpful recognition. The player UI does not reveal conversion percentages, curves or an optimal price.

For future seasons, loyalty, advertising, inflation or local economics, extend the centralized configuration and explicit `demandFor` inputs, then capture the resulting context at day opening. Competitor pricing can similarly extend the existing diversion calculation. Do not add separate price checks to the renderer, customer routes, service loop or accounting.

Tests cover every curve point, interpolation, temperature/quality boundaries, monotonicity, sensitivity in both directions, cumulative/capped branding, hard cutoffs, recipe/weather application once, and seeded samples of 100,000 visitors per scenario. Browser tests exercise zero-demand days, actual queues/receipts/inventory, saved-day resumption, education and unchanged bank balances. Expected revenue/profit comparisons use ordinary cup revenue and prepared-batch costs, without artificial price bonuses.
